# Frequently Asked Questions About Me

These questions and answers describe my academic background, technical interests, projects, learning preferences, opinions, and goals. The answers are written in the first person to represent my own perspective.

# Category 2: Learning Style and Problem-Solving

### Q6. Why do I prefer understanding the reason behind a technical command instead of simply copying it?

I believe copying a command may solve an immediate problem, but it does not guarantee that I understand the solution. If I know why a command is being used, I can recognize similar situations in the future and make better decisions independently. This is particularly important in programming, database management, and software configuration, where a small mistake can affect the entire implementation.

### Q7. What happens when I receive too many technical instructions at once, and how do I prefer the explanation to be structured?

When several unfamiliar steps are explained together, it can become difficult for me to identify which action should be performed first. I prefer instructions divided into manageable stages. I like completing one step, checking its result, and then moving to the next. This approach makes troubleshooting easier and reduces confusion.

### Q8. How do I approach a technical error when the application or command does not produce the expected result?

I prefer identifying the actual cause instead of randomly changing different settings. I first examine the error message and consider whether the issue is related to code, configuration, permissions, dependencies, or the environment. Then I test a possible solution and check whether the expected result appears. Understanding why the error occurred is more useful to me than only making it disappear.

### Q9. Why do I consider practical implementation more useful than memorizing technical definitions?

Definitions are important for understanding terminology, but practical implementation shows how a concept behaves in an actual system. For example, understanding database permissions becomes clearer when I create users, assign roles, and test what each user can access. I remember concepts better when I have performed the work myself.

### Q10. What kind of explanation helps me move from being dependent on instructions to solving technical tasks independently?

I benefit from explanations that first establish the purpose of a task, then demonstrate the steps, and finally explain the reasoning behind the result. Once I understand the process, I want to try similar tasks without following every instruction. My goal is to gradually develop independent problem-solving ability.

# Category 3: AI, Language Models, and Technology

### Q11. What makes me interested in exploring local language models instead of relying entirely on online AI services?

I am interested in understanding how language models can run on a local computer and communicate with other applications. My LM Studio experiment introduced me to local model execution and Python API integration. Local execution also raises interesting questions about hardware requirements, privacy, model size, and performance. My own computer has limited resources, so I am interested in understanding how smaller quantized models make local experimentation possible.

### Q12. What did I learn from running Llama 3.2 1B Instruct through LM Studio on my computer?

I learned how to download and load a quantized language model in LM Studio and communicate with it through a local API using Python. I also explored an embedding model and tested whether a Python script could successfully send a request and receive a response. This practical experiment helped me understand the connection between a local LLM server and an external Python application.

### Q13. Why do I believe an AI system should acknowledge uncertainty instead of always producing a confident answer?

An AI system may not always have sufficient evidence to answer a question correctly. If it generates a confident answer without proper support, users may trust incorrect information. I believe a reliable system should recognize its limitations, explain when information is insufficient, and avoid inventing details. This is particularly important in systems that support decisions affecting people.

### Q14. How do I distinguish between using AI as a learning assistant and depending on AI to complete all my work?

I consider AI useful when it helps me understand concepts, troubleshoot errors, explore ideas, and learn new tools. However, I do not want to copy generated answers without understanding them. I prefer asking why a solution works, checking the output, and learning how to implement the task myself. My objective is to use AI to improve my understanding rather than replace my learning process.

### Q15. Which aspects of AI development interest me beyond simply generating answers from a language model?

I am interested in how AI systems retrieve information, process evidence, represent uncertainty, and communicate their results. I also find the connection between language models, embeddings, databases, and application interfaces interesting. I want to understand how individual components combine to form a useful software system.

# Category 4: Projects and Technical Decisions

### Q16. How does my EatEasy project demonstrate my understanding of user-centered design rather than only visual decoration?

EatEasy was an HCI project in which I designed a food delivery application prototype containing nine screens in Figma. My work involved arranging interface elements, selecting colors, maintaining consistency, and considering the user journey. The purpose was not simply to make attractive screens but to organize the interface so that users could understand and navigate the application more easily.

### Q17. Why did I choose a student skill exchange concept for my SkillBridge database project, and what makes its data relationships important?

SkillBridge addresses the idea of students sharing skills with one another. A student can offer a skill and also request to learn a different skill. This creates several related records that need to be managed consistently. Users, Skills, Offered_Skills, Wanted_Skills, Exchange_Request, Sessions, Reviews, and Exchange_History represent different parts of the platform. Their relationships are important because they allow the database to track who offers a skill, who wants to learn it, and how an exchange progresses.

### Q18. What is the central problem my Exam Integrity System intends to address?

My FYP focuses on supporting the planning of student seating arrangements before physical examinations. During this process, situations such as hall overcapacity, duplicate roll number allocation, or hall unavailability may occur. However, these observations do not always mean that an arrangement is completely invalid. My project explores how AI can assist in evaluating hall-related information, identifying potential conflicts, recognizing uncertainty, and routing unclear or conflicting cases to a human reviewer, such as an admin or invigilator.

### Q19. Why is uncertainty handling important in my Exam Integrity System?

Not every detected issue automatically means that a seating arrangement is invalid. For example, a hall may appear overcapacity because of incomplete or outdated information. Similarly, duplicate roll number allocation may need verification before a final decision is made. I want the system to distinguish clear conflicts from uncertain situations and involve a human reviewer when the available evidence is insufficient. This can help reduce incorrect decisions and make the system more reliable.

### Q20. How do my different projects demonstrate that I am interested in both building software and understanding the systems behind it?

My projects involve different levels of software development. EatEasy focuses on the user interface, SkillBridge focuses on relational database design, and my FYP explores AI-assisted decision support. My LM Studio experiment involves language model integration, while my n8n experiment focuses on connecting software services. Together, they show my interest in understanding both the visible application and the technical systems that support it.

# Category 5: Opinions About Responsible and Useful Technology

### Q21. Why do I think an unusual user action should not automatically be interpreted as suspicious behavior?

An unusual action does not always have a suspicious intention. The same behavior may occur for different reasons depending on the situation. In examination proctoring, for example, looking away from your paper during physical exam is not enough evidence to conclude that a student is cheating. I believe decisions should consider context, repeated patterns, and evidence reliability.

### Q22. What makes an AI-generated decision difficult to trust when the system cannot explain the evidence behind it?

If a system produces a decision without showing the evidence or reasoning behind it, users cannot properly evaluate whether the result is justified. This makes it difficult to identify mistakes and challenge incorrect decisions. I prefer AI systems that provide understandable explanations and make their limitations visible.

### Q23. How should a software system behave when the available information is incomplete or contradictory?

I believe the system should avoid pretending that the information is sufficient. It should identify the missing or conflicting evidence and communicate that the result is uncertain. Depending on the situation, it can request more information or send the case for human review. A system should not force a conclusion just to produce an output.

### Q24. Why do I believe a technically advanced application can still fail if its user interface is poorly designed?

Technology is useful only when people can interact with it effectively. If an application has confusing navigation, unclear instructions, or poorly organized information, users may struggle even when its backend works correctly. This is why I consider usability and functionality equally important when thinking about software applications.

### Q25. What kind of balance do I want between automation and human involvement in important software decisions?

I believe repetitive tasks can often be automated, but important decisions should consider the consequences of mistakes. Automation can improve efficiency and identify patterns, while humans can interpret context that a system may not understand. In uncertain situations, combining automated assistance with human judgment is a more responsible approach.
