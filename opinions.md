# My Opinions, Personality, and Way of Thinking

## 1. How I Think and Approach Problems

I am someone who prefers understanding a problem before jumping directly to its solution. When I encounter something unfamiliar, I usually want to know what the actual problem is, why it exists, and what possible approaches can solve it.

I do not find it very helpful to receive instructions without understanding their purpose. For example, when working with SQL queries, programming code, or software tools, I want to understand what each command does and why it is necessary.

I also prefer breaking complicated problems into smaller parts. When everything is explained at once, it can become difficult to identify which step should be performed first. A structured approach makes technical work easier for me.

## 2. My Learning Philosophy

I believe practical understanding is more valuable than memorizing information only for exams or assignments.

Reading theoretical material is useful, but I understand concepts better when I can see them working in a real situation. I prefer examples that connect technical concepts with everyday applications.

For example, when studying database management, I understand relationships more clearly when they are explained through an actual website scenario rather than through isolated definitions.

Similarly, when learning programming, I prefer writing and running code, observing the output, identifying errors, and understanding how to fix them.

My goal is not just to complete a task but to understand enough to perform similar tasks independently in the future.

## 3. My Opinion About Artificial Intelligence

I find artificial intelligence one of the most interesting areas of computer science because it connects programming, data, reasoning, and practical problem-solving.

I am particularly interested in how AI can be used in real-world systems rather than being limited to theoretical concepts.

However, I do not believe that every AI-generated result should automatically be considered correct. AI systems can make mistakes, misunderstand situations, or produce answers that sound confident despite having insufficient evidence.

I believe AI should be developed with attention to accuracy, transparency, and its limitations. A useful AI system should be able to acknowledge when it does not know something instead of inventing an answer.

## 4. My View About Human Decision-Making and AI

I believe AI should assist human decision-making, especially in situations where a decision can affect someone.

An AI system should not always make a final decision simply because it has detected an unusual pattern.

For example, in remote online examination proctoring, a student looking away from the screen does not necessarily mean that the student is cheating. There may be different explanations for the same behavior.

In my opinion, such systems should consider the available evidence and its reliability. If the evidence is unclear, the case should be referred to a human reviewer instead of automatically declaring the student guilty.

This is one of the main ideas behind my interest in uncertainty-aware AI systems.

## 5. My Interest in UI/UX Design

I believe a good user interface should make an application easier to understand and operate.

A visually attractive design is not necessarily a good design if users cannot understand its navigation or complete their tasks comfortably.

While working on UI/UX projects, I became interested in screen organization, visual consistency, color selection, user journeys, and the overall experience of users.

I think designers should consider the needs of actual users before deciding how an application should look.

I am still developing my design skills and want to improve my understanding of usability, interaction design, and practical prototyping.

## 6. My Working Style and Preferences

I prefer a clear and organized working process. When starting a project, I like to understand its requirements first and then divide the work into smaller tasks.

I usually prefer:

- Step-by-step technical guidance.
- Practical examples instead of explanations based only on definitions.
- Clear instructions about which tool to use and why.
- Understanding errors instead of simply receiving corrected code.
- Organized project files and meaningful documentation.
- Honest feedback about mistakes and areas that need improvement.

I do not want someone to agree with everything I say just to make me feel comfortable. If my approach is incorrect, I prefer being told directly, along with an explanation of what needs to change.

## 7. My Opinion About Problem-Solving

I believe errors are a normal part of technical learning. When something does not work, the important thing is to identify the reason instead of repeatedly trying random solutions.

I prefer checking one issue at a time. This helps me understand whether the problem is related to code, configuration, dependencies, permissions, or something else.

I also believe that a solution should be practical for the available resources. For example, when working with local language models, hardware limitations such as RAM and processing capability should be considered before selecting a model.

## 8. My Career and Professional Interests

I am interested in exploring different areas of computer science to understand where my abilities and interests can develop further.

Some areas that attract my attention are:

- Artificial intelligence and language models.
- UI/UX and user-centered design.
- Python programming.
- Workflow automation.
- Database management.
- Software testing and quality.
- Practical applications of emerging technologies.

I do not consider myself an expert in all these areas. I am a student who is exploring them through coursework, experiments, and projects.

## 9. My Perspective on Continuous Improvement

I believe learning should be an ongoing process. Technology changes frequently, so depending only on previously learned information is not enough.

I want to keep improving my technical abilities, become more confident in implementing projects, and develop the ability to solve problems without depending entirely on step-by-step instructions.

For me, progress means gradually moving from understanding a concept to applying it independently.

## 10. What I Expect From an AI Assistant

I prefer an AI assistant that acts like a practical learning partner.

It should explain concepts clearly, identify mistakes, provide relevant examples, and avoid making unsupported assumptions about my knowledge or experience.

When information about me is not available in its knowledge base, it should acknowledge that limitation instead of creating personal details.

I also prefer answers that are relevant to the actual question rather than unnecessarily complicated explanations.
