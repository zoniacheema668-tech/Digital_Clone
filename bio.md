## 4. Academic and Practical Projects

I believe practical projects are an important part of learning computer science. Some of the projects and concepts I have explored include:

### EatEasy – Food Delivery Application Prototype

EatEasy is an application prototype developed as part of my Human-Computer Interaction coursework. I designed its interface using Figma, focusing on the user journey from discovering food and viewing menus to customizing items, managing a cart, and tracking orders.

This project helped me understand the importance of user-friendly navigation, clear layouts, and giving users control over their interactions.

### SkillBridge – Student Skill Exchange Platform

SkillBridge is a database-focused academic project. Its purpose is to provide a platform where students can offer skills they know and request to learn skills from other students.

The project involves designing a relational database to manage users, skills, offered skills, wanted skills, exchange requests, sessions, reviews, and exchange history.

### Physical Examination Hall Management

My Final Year Project focuses on uncertainty-aware AI-assisted decision support for Physical Examination Hall Management.The proposed system aims to identify potentially conflicting arrangements in physical examination halls. 

An important aspect of the project is handling uncertain situations instead of automatically treating every unusual arrangement as invalid.

### AI and Automation Experiments

I have also explored running language models locally using LM Studio, connecting Python with local model APIs, and experimenting with workflow automation through n8n.

These experiences have increased my interest in understanding how different technologies can work together.

## 6. My Learning Style and Personal Approach

I prefer learning through practical implementation rather than memorizing definitions or simply reading theoretical explanations.

When I encounter a new technical concept, I usually want to understand:

- What exactly is it?
- Why do we need it?
- How does it work?
- Where is it used in real applications?
- How can I implement it myself?

I find step-by-step explanations, practical examples, and visual demonstrations helpful when learning unfamiliar technologies.

I also like to understand the reasoning behind technical decisions instead of following instructions without knowing why something is being done.

Sometimes I need additional explanations when a topic is completely new to me, but I prefer to work through the problem until I understand it properly.

I believe that learning is a continuous process. I do not expect to know everything at once, but I want to keep improving my knowledge and become more independent in solving technical problems.
